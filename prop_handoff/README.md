# PropHandoff

Blender add-on (4.2 → 5.x) to animate **props held by a character** without
handling constraints and keyframes by hand.

- **Section 1 — Transfers**: the prop follows a hand. *Assign → slot*,
  *Release*, *Throw* at the current frame, without a visual jump; navigable
  history rebuilt from the F-Curves.
- **Section 2 — Two-Hand Grip**: the hands follow the prop (long object). *Grip
  Zone* (proximity blend driven by drivers, no dependency cycle), keyframed
  *Attach* of both hands, *Master Hand* (the prop follows one hand, the other
  hand follows the prop).

Tested in **Blender 5.2** (slotted actions) on an **Auto-Rig Pro** rig
(`c_hand_ik.l` / `c_hand_ik.r`, no parent, with the rig's own `Child Of →
c_traj`) and on a **Rigify** human generated by the Rigify bundled with Blender
(`hand_ik.L` / `hand_ik.R`) — see `tests/` and "Supported rigs".

---

## Installation

1. Build the zip: `python build_zip.py` (or `--bump` to increment the patch
   number). The zip contains the `prop_handoff/` **folder**.
2. Blender → *Edit ▸ Preferences ▸ Add-ons ▸ ▾ ▸ Install from Disk…* → pick
   `prop_handoff_v<X.Y.Z>.zip`, tick **PropHandoff**.
3. Update: disable, *Remove*, **restart Blender** (the modules stay in
   memory), reinstall.

The **PropHandoff** tab appears in the sidebar (`N`) of the 3D View.

Everything the add-on creates is native Blender data (constraints, empties,
drivers, custom properties): it is saved in the `.blend` and survives a
restart, even with the add-on disabled (the panel is then missing, the rig is
not). Every driver expression is a "simple expression": no *Auto Run Python
Scripts* needed.

## Getting started

### The active prop

The **Active prop** field (eyedropper) pins the object the add-on works on.
Essential: to pick bones you then select the armature in Pose mode, and the
prop must not change because of that. Empty, the active prop is the active
object of the view.

Every object that already carries PropHandoff data (slots, IK targets, grip
points) appears as a **tab** under the field, in both panels: one click
switches the whole panel to that prop, in any mode (Object, Pose…), without
touching the selection. The eyedropper is only needed the first time, to
bring a new object in. Several props can be held at the same time (a knife in
one hand, a sword in the other): each one has its own slots, constraints and
history, and transfers of different props may share the same frame.

### Section 1 — Transfers (ball, sword…)

1. Eyedropper on the prop → **Setup Prop**. Two default slots (`Hand_L`,
   `Hand_R`), one `Child Of` constraint per slot (`PH_Hand_L`…), all at
   influence 0. The selected armature (or the only armature of the scene) is
   targeted, the hands' deform bones detected if they exist (`hand.L`,
   `hand.l`, `DEF-hand.L`…).
2. **Slots**: target (armature + bone, or object) of each slot; **From
   Selection** takes the active bone (Pose mode).
3. At the wanted frame: **Assign → Left hand**. The prop does not move by a
   pixel, it is now attached. Later: **Assign → Right hand**, **Release**,
   **Throw…** (parabola extending the gesture; adjustable afterwards with
   `F9`).
4. **Transfers**: history (`Frame N → slot`), *Previous / Next / Go*.

### Section 2 — Two-Hand Grip (spear, rifle, bat)

1. **IK Targets**: the armature then the IK bone of each hand (`c_hand_ik.l` /
   `c_hand_ik.r` on Auto-Rig Pro, `hand_ik.L` / `hand_ik.R` on Rigify — arms in
   IK mode), or a plain object (empty bone field). Warnings: a `WGT-…` widget
   picked instead of the rig; a duplicate rig that deforms no mesh.
2. Put the hands on the shaft, then **Setup Two-Hand Grip**: the grip points
   (`Grip_L`, `Grip_R`, children of the prop) are placed on the hands, the
   `GripZone_<prop>` sphere shows the radius. **Set left/right hand pose**
   repositions a grip later.
3. **Grip Zone ▸ Enable**: each hand progressively snaps to its grip when it
   enters the sphere (influence 1 under 5 cm, 0 at the radius). Continuous,
   non-keyframed effect: move the spear a little, the hands follow without
   lag; move a hand away, it lets go; bring it back, it grabs again. A **big**
   move of the spear makes the hands let go (it is a proximity blend) — for a
   firm hold, use the attach or the Lock.
4. **Attach ▸ Attach Both Hands**: keyframed hold (`Child Of PH_TwoHand_L/R`
   on the IK targets → grips). The hands stay exactly where they are and then
   follow the spear. **Release Both Hands**: back to normal IK control, without
   a jump (nothing re-grabs). **→ Switch to Two-Handed**: the prop is already
   in one hand (section 1), the other one is added. In all three cases the
   past effect of the Grip Zone / Lock is **baked to keys** on the hand before
   its constraint is removed: the motion already animated does not change
   (`Disable` offers the same option, ticked by default).
5. **Master Hand ▸ Left / Right**: the prop follows this hand; the other hand
   follows it through its Grip Zone (restored if a previous master hand had
   removed it), or through a keyframed attach if the Grip Zone is not wanted.
   **None**: the prop becomes free again and both hands get their Grip Zone
   back. You can therefore switch Left → Right → None freely, Lock included
   (the master hand loses its zone, the other one stays locked).
6. **Grip Zone ▸ Lock**: the zone is ignored, the hands stay on the grips
   whatever the distance (keyed at the current frame, `Unlock` to return to
   the blend). The Lock also installs the **arm reach**: the spear **stops**
   when an arm is fully extended — it does not leave the hands, it simply
   cannot go further. Adjustable margin (2 % by default). Enables the Grip
   Zone itself if needed.

Every displayed state (dots, "Frame N: …", pressed buttons) is read from the
constraints, never from a separate variable. Single exception, an intention:
`ph_grip_zone_wanted` (1 after Enable/Lock, 0 after Disable/Release/Attach)
tells Master Hand and "None" whether the Grip Zone must be restored.

Without auto-key, a move made with the mouse only exists until the next frame
change — which the operators trigger internally (bake). Every operator
therefore starts by **freezing** the unkeyed moves of the prop and of the
hands (hold at `frame − 1`, key at `frame`): the pose you see when you click
is the one that counts.

## Design rules

Born from nine test iterations; each one cost a real bug.

### Transfer without a jump (`operators.apply_handoff`)

1. **Visual** matrix of the prop (constraints applied, depsgraph evaluated).
2. **Hold** keys at `frame − 1` as `CONSTANT`, on the influences **and** on
   loc/rot/scale, with the value **evaluated from the F-Curve** at that frame
   (never the current value: at the transfer frame the channels change space,
   world → bone local, and Blender would interpolate between two spaces).
3. Every `PH_*` influence to 0 at the frame; the target slot's to 1.
4. Compensation on the **basis** (animatable): `basis = (target @ inverse)⁻¹ @
   world`. `inverse_matrix` stays identity and `set_inverse_pending = False`:
   Blender's deferred computation would happen after the operator, and the
   inverse is not animatable.
5. Interpolation always forced explicitly: Blender copies the interpolation
   of the **previous key onto every new key** (measured in 5.2), so a
   transfer key inserted after the CONSTANT hold key would be CONSTANT too —
   and so would every key the animator inserts afterwards (stepped motion
   after each transfer, fixed in 0.3.2). Transfer and bake keys therefore
   get the user's "New Interpolation Type" preference explicitly; the key is
   found by its `co.x`, never by `keyframe_points[-1]`.

### Compatibility layer (`utils.fcurve_collection`)

In 5.x `Action.fcurves` **no longer exists**: every access goes through
`layers → strips → channelbag(action_slot)`, with a fallback on the historical
API. Objects and pose bones share the same code through `owner.id_data` /
`owner.path_from_id(...)`; object and bone constraints through
`constraint.path_from_id("influence")`.

### Data

The slot list lives in a `CollectionProperty` and its JSON mirror
`obj["ph_slots"]` (ID properties refuse lists of dicts and strings in 3.6);
`ph_grip_points` likewise; `ph_grip_zone_radius` stays a **native float** read
by the drivers. Blender may suffix a name (`Grip_L.001`): the real name is
always stored and re-read.

## Grip Zone: what the depsgraph does, and the architecture kept

A driver on `pose.bones["B"].constraints["X"].influence` feeds
`BONE_LOCAL(B)`. Any external object that reads B one way or another and feeds
that driver closes the graph. Three architectures failed before the right one:
direct bone ↔ prop distance, probe driven by Transforms, probe driven by Single
Property. **Do not try them again.**

Architecture kept: **reverse the measurement**. The driver itself reads the
`location` channels of its bone (depsgraph exemption), and it is the grip point
that is expressed in the frame of those channels by two empties that never
depend on the bone:

- `PH_Frame_<S>`: frame of the channels. For a bone B with parent P,
  `F = arm @ pose(P) @ rest(P)⁻¹ @ rest(B)`; a bone-parented object being
  placed at the **tail** of the bone, `matrix_parent_inverse = T(0, −len_P, 0)
  @ rest(P)⁻¹ @ rest(B)`. Without parent: `parent_type = OBJECT`,
  `parent_inverse = rest(B)`. Bones without **Local Location**
  (`use_local_location` off: Rigify's IK controllers, which move in their
  parent's axes): Blender applies `loc` at B's head but in P's orientation —
  only the translation of `rest(P)⁻¹ @ rest(B)` is kept. Verified numerically
  (5 random configurations per case, 1e-5); before 0.2.3 the Grip Zone on
  Rigify measured a wrong distance (influence 0.16 with the hand on the grip).
- `PH_GripLocal_<S>`: child of the frame, `Copy Location` towards the grip;
  its *Local Space* position is `F⁻¹ @ grip_world`.
- Expression (min/max/sqrt only → no "Auto Run Python Scripts"):
  `max(0.0, min(1.0, 1.0 - (s * sqrt((bx-gx)*(bx-gx) + (by-gy)*(by-gy) + (bz-gz)*(bz-gz)) - 0.05) / max(radius - 0.05, 0.0001)))`

### Lock and arm reach

`ph_grip_lock` (keyframable custom property of the prop) enters the Grip Zone
driver: `max(lock, proximity)`. At 1 the hands are on the grips whatever the
distance; the Lock is therefore an animated state like the attach, without an
extra constraint on the hands.

So that the spear stops at the end of the arms, the prop carries a **Limit
Location `PH_Reach`** (world space) whose `min = max` bounds are driven:
`position = prop channels − correction`, influence = `lock × gate`.

- Measured (5.2.1): a driver on `delta_location` **reads back its own output**
  (for an object, *Transform Space* = `location + delta_location`), hence
  oscillating values; a constraint, on the other hand, is never read back by
  the drivers of the same object. And an object can only read its own channels
  without a cycle from its own drivers ("same ID" exemption): the whole
  computation chain therefore lives on the prop, as driven custom properties
  (`ph_rg_*`, `ph_rd_*`, `ph_rf_*`, `ph_rc_*`), without cycle or lag (verified
  frame by frame).
- External inputs: the `PH_ReachHelper_L/R` helpers, bone-parented to the
  **shoulder** (see "Supported rigs"), placed at the root of the IK chain. The
  root itself (`arm_ik.*`) depends on the IK target: reading it would create a
  cycle.
- Reach = (upper arm + forearm) × world scale of the rig × (1 − margin).
- Per hand: grip rotated by the prop rotation (Euler XYZ read whatever the
  rotation mode, scale included), distance to the root, factor
  `max(0, d − reach) / d`. The translation is computed by **alternating
  projections** onto the two reach spheres (`REACH_ROUNDS` rounds, one pass
  per side, `ph_rp<k>*` properties): each pass projects the previous
  translation onto one sphere, so the result is continuous (1-Lipschitz) in
  the prop's channels — the stop never jumps. Picking "the hand furthest
  beyond its reach" (0.2.x) switched abruptly when both hands were out of
  reach in different directions (pushing the spear backwards: measured
  0.57 u jumps).
- **Master Hand under Lock** (0.3.2): the prop's Limit Location is gated off
  (the prop follows the controller), so the stop moves to the **master
  controller**: driver-free `Limit Distance` constraints (inside a sphere,
  world space) `PH_ReachMaster_self` → own shoulder helper, distance = own
  reach, and `PH_ReachMaster_other` → `PH_ReachTarget_<other>`, a helper at
  "other shoulder − a" (Copy Location of the other shoulder helper plus the
  world offset −a, a = other grip − controller measured when the master
  hand is set), distance = other reach — the other hand's grip stays within
  its arm's reach. Both are repeated once. Their influence is **keyframed**
  with the Lock (a driver reading the prop would link the controller to the
  object it drives); the controllers never read the prop: no cycle. Baked to
  keys and removed when the master hand changes or is set to None.
- `ph_reach_gate`: gate keyed to 0 by `apply_handoff()` as soon as a slot is
  active (the prop follows a hand: the Limit Location no longer makes sense),
  1 on release. The gate is closed **before** the compensation and reopened
  **after**: otherwise the constraint pins the prop during the residual
  computation and the recorded channels are wrong ("the spear goes anywhere"
  bug in Master Hand). In Master Hand the Lock stays active for the other
  hand; the master hand loses its Grip Zone (anti-cycle) after its effect is
  baked up to the current frame.
- The prop must have **no parent** (measurement in world space). Functions
  min/max/sqrt/sin/cos and the conditional only: no autoexec.

### Supported rigs

The add-on only reads controllers and constraints; the quality of the skinning
does not concern it. What it expects from a rig, and what is adapted per rig:

- **IK chain (arm reach)** — `utils.find_ik_chain` accepts an IK constraint
  that targets the controller (Auto-Rig Pro: `c_hand_ik.l`) **or a bone that
  depends on it** (Rigify: `MCH-upper_arm_ik_target.L`, child of `hand_ik.L`).
  The "shoulder" carrying the reach helper is the first ancestor of the chain
  root that depends neither on the controller nor on the IK target:
  `c_shoulder.l` on ARP; on Rigify, `MCH-upper_arm_ik_swing.L` (Damped Track
  towards the target) is skipped in favour of `MCH-upper_arm_parent.L`. Pure
  FK rig or object IK target: Lock without reach, "Arm reach not installed"
  message.
- **Dependencies between bones** — `utils.bone_depends_on` rebuilds the graph
  statically (parenting, constraint targets — a whole IK chain depends on its
  target and its pole —, bones read by the drivers), with the same rule as the
  depsgraph: influence does not count. A slot whose target **depends** on a
  hand (deform bone `hand.l` / `DEF-hand.L` copying the controller, object
  parented to the hand…) is treated like a slot targeting that hand: suspended
  while the hand follows the prop, baked to keys then removed before the hand
  follows it, and *Assign* on that slot first removes the Grip Zone or the
  attach of that hand (`utils.target_depends_on_handle`).
- **Default slots** (*Setup Prop*) — the hand's deform bone, case-insensitive:
  `hand.L`, `DEF-hand.L`, `Hand_L`, `left_hand`, `hand_l` (hence ARP's
  `hand.l`). It follows the hand in FK as in IK; thanks to the previous point
  it cannot close a cycle with section 2.
- **Third-party constraints on the controllers** (ARP's `Child Of → c_traj`,
  Rigify's `MCH-hand_ik.parent.L` parent switch): transfers and attaches stay
  exact (residual correction, below).

### ⚠️ Deviations measured from the rebuild specification

Two points of the specification were **contradicted by measurement** in
Blender 5.2.1 (depsgraph log captured by `tests/headless_tests.py`), and a
third one completed. The implementation follows the measurement.

| Measured case | "Dependency cycle detected"? |
|---|---|
| **Single Property** driver `pose.bones["B"].location[i]` on the same bone | **yes** (3 cycles per hand, one per component) |
| **Transform Channel** driver on the same bone, *Transform Space* | **no** — correct animated values |
| `Child Of` hand → grip at **influence 0**, prop following the hand | **yes** |
| Same, `enabled = False` | **yes** |
| `Copy Transforms` disabled, **without driver** | **yes** |
| Target removed / constraint removed | no |

1. **`bx by bz` bone variables: Transform Channel, not Single Property.**
   `location[i]` is linked to `BONE_LOCAL(B)`, which the driver also feeds:
   two-node cycle. The `is_same_bone_dependency` exemption only covers the
   `BONE_DONE(B) → BONE_LOCAL(B)` relation of the same bone — exactly the one
   of a Transform Channel variable targeting B. In *Transform Space* it reads
   the loc channels (`BKE_pchan_to_mat4`), animated and without constraints.
   For an object, same variable, "same ID" exemption.
2. **A constraint keeps its relations whatever its influence or activation.**
   "Release" (influence 0) or "disable" never cuts a cycle; only removal does.
   The add-on therefore maintains the invariant **one direction per hand** at
   the level of the *present* constraints:
   - `prop → hand` (`PH_<slot>` slot targeting the IK handle, or a bone /
     object depending on it) and `hand → prop` (`PH_TwoHand_*`,
     `PH_GripBlend_*` targeting a grip of this prop) never coexist on the same
     hand;
   - before the prop follows a hand (*Assign*, *Master Hand*, *Switch to
     Two-Handed*), that hand's Grip Zone is removed and its attach, if it has
     a history, is **baked to keys** then removed
     (`operators.bake_and_remove_constraint`);
   - before a hand follows the prop (*Attach*, *Grip Zone*), the dormant slot
     targeting it is baked to keys on the prop then removed; while the hand
     follows the prop, that slot is **suspended** (`utils.slot_is_suspended`):
     `ensure_constraints` does not recreate its constraint, the list shows it,
     and *Assign* lifts the suspension;
   - the bake is the equivalent of *Bake Action ▸ Visual Keying ▸ Clear
     Constraints* limited to one constraint and to the frames where it acts:
     the motion is preserved identically (verified at 1e-4), the operator
     report gives the frames concerned.
3. **Compensation with residual correction** (`operators.settle_owner`). The
   formula `basis = C⁻¹ @ world` assumes no other constraint acts on the
   owner. On Auto-Rig Pro, `c_hand_ik.*` carries a `Child Of → c_traj`; as
   soon as `c_traj` moves, the direct placement jumps. After the keys are
   inserted, the result is evaluated, the residual measured and corrected (at
   most twice; exact for a constraint that pre-multiplies by a constant
   matrix). Without a third-party constraint iteration 0 is enough and nothing
   changes.

The specification protocol (§14) passes entirely with these three corrections,
on the synthetic scene, on the real Auto-Rig Pro rig and on a generated Rigify
human, clean console.

## Tests

Two headless scripts write their report (Blender's stdout/stderr included,
hence the depsgraph messages) into `tests/*.log`:

```powershell
# synthetic scene: ~260 checks (jumps, hold, history, frame, cycles, bake, Lock,
# bones without Local Location, dependencies, Rigify-like IK chain…)
powershell -File tests/run_headless.ps1

# §14 protocol on a real .blend (Auto-Rig Pro rig: 62 checks)
powershell -File tests/run_rig_protocol.ps1 -Blend "path\to\scene.blend"

# §14 protocol on a Rigify human generated on the fly (62 checks)
powershell -File tests/run_rig_protocol.ps1 -Rigify
```

Other rig: `PH_ARMATURE`, `PH_BONE_L`, `PH_BONE_R` as environment variables.

Without PowerShell: `blender --background --factory-startup --python
tests/headless_tests.py`. The cycle detection is itself checked by a deliberate
cycle at the end of the script.

## Structure

```
prop_handoff/
├── __init__.py    bl_info + register()/unregister() (hot reload)
├── utils.py       pure functions: F-Curves, keys, matrices, drivers, JSON,
│                  anti-cycle rules, static bone dependencies — no Blender
│                  class
├── operators.py   PropertyGroups + 24 operators + core: apply_handoff,
│                  settle_owner, bake_and_remove_constraint, attach_hand,
│                  release_hand, build_helpers
└── panel.py       UIList + 11 panels (PropHandoff tab)
build_zip.py       installable zip ("/" separators, no __pycache__, CRC checked)
tests/             headless tests (see above)
```

`utils.py` never imports the other two modules. Whatever does not start with
`PH_` belongs to the animator and is never touched.

## Known limitations

- Arm reach: translation-only correction (the spear slides on the reach
  spheres, it does not rotate); prop without parent; bone IK targets of an
  armature whose IK constraint targets the bone or a bone depending on it
  (nothing for object IK targets nor for a pure FK arm). In Master Hand the
  other hand's stop uses the grip offset measured when the master hand was
  set (refreshed by *Set pose*): a translation of the master hand keeps it
  exact, a rotation makes it approximate. If the rig uses *IK stretch* (Rigify:
  `IK_Stretch` = 1 by default), the spear still stops at the rest length. The
  reach helper follows the shoulder: an animated translation of a bone located
  between the shoulder and the chain root is not followed.
- The bone dependency analysis is static and conservative: a slot may be
  "suspended" while a zero-influence driver would make the cycle harmless —
  which is also what the depsgraph would do.
- The `PH_Frame_*` frame follows the hierarchical parenting of the bone: the
  controller's own constraints (Auto-Rig Pro IK space switch) are not
  reproduced in the **distance measurement** of the Grip Zone. With `c_traj`
  at rest it is exact; moved, the zone shifts by as much. Attaches and
  transfers, on the other hand, stay exact (residual correction).
- The Grip Zone is a continuous effect (driver): when an operation ends it
  (attach, release, Master Hand, Disable), its effect is baked to dense keys
  on the hand up to the current frame, then the constraint disappears.
- `Copy Transforms` copies the grip's scale: apply the prop's scale (`Ctrl+A ▸
  Scale`), the add-on warns.
- Two transfers on two consecutive frames step on each other (hold keys at
  `frame − 1`): leave at least two frames between them.
- The throw overwrites the position keys over the flight duration; no
  collision.
- Linked (library) objects are refused: *Make Local* or an override first.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| The prop jumps on assignment | A non-`PH_` constraint also acts on the object and is not a constant pre-multiplication; or `Set Inverse` was clicked in the constraint panel. Assign again. |
| "Dependency cycle detected" in the console | Constraints edited by hand: a slot targets a hand carrying `PH_TwoHand_*` / `PH_GripBlend_*`. The red box reports it; running *Assign*, *Master Hand* or *Attach* again cleans it. |
| The hands let go when the spear moves | Normal for the Grip Zone (proximity). Use *Attach*, the *Lock*, or raise the radius. |
| `Assign → Hand_R` greyed out | The slot has no target. |
| Slot shown as suspended (broken-link icon) | That hand follows the prop (section 2), or the slot targets a bone / object depending on it. *Assign* lifts the suspension (and bakes/removes the attach or the Grip Zone). |
| "Arm reach not installed" after Lock | No IK constraint targets the IK bone nor a bone depending on it (pure FK arm, object IK target): the Lock works, the spear does not stop. |
| The hands do not grab by proximity on Rigify | Version earlier than 0.2.3 (frame of bones without *Local Location*). |
| The prop or the hands move in steps after a transfer | Version earlier than 0.3.2 (new keys inherited the CONSTANT hold key). Select the keys in the Graph Editor and set their interpolation to Bezier. |
| In Master Hand the spear slips out of the other hand | Version earlier than 0.3.2 (no stop on the master controller). |
| The panel is gone after a restart | The add-on is disabled: tick it again in the preferences (keep *Auto-Save Preferences* on). The rig itself is still in the file. |
